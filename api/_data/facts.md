## Indian wedding traditions (glossary)
These are general definitions. Not every tradition listed is necessarily part of this couple's ceremony.
- **Sangeet**: a festive pre-wedding night of music, dance performances by family and friends, food, and dancing.
- **Haldi**: a pre-wedding ceremony where family and friends apply turmeric paste to the bride and groom for good luck and a pre-wedding glow. Guests often wear yellow or pastels.
- **Ganesh Puja**: a prayer to Lord Ganesha, the remover of obstacles, held to bless the start of the wedding festivities.
- **Mehndi / Henna**: intricate temporary designs painted on hands with henna paste. Henna artists are available at the Sangeet.
- **Baraat**: the groom's joyful procession to the wedding venue, with music and dancing. Guests are encouraged to join in and dance!
- **Milni**: the formal welcome when the bride's family greets the groom's family after the baraat.
- **Mandap**: the decorated canopy where the wedding ceremony takes place.
- **Pandit / Priest**: the officiant who leads the Hindu ceremony and its Sanskrit prayers.
- **Jai Mala / Varmala**: the exchange of flower garlands between the bride and groom.
- **Kanyadaan**: the bride's parents give their daughter's hand in marriage, a deeply emotional moment.
- **Mangalsutra**: a sacred necklace the groom ties around the bride's neck as a symbol of marriage.
- **Sindoor**: red vermilion powder the groom applies to the bride's hair parting.
- **Pheras / Saptapadi**: the couple walks around a sacred fire, making seven vows to each other. This is the heart of the ceremony.
- **Agni**: the sacred fire that serves as witness to the vows.
- **Vidaai**: the bride's emotional farewell to her family at the end of the wedding.
- **Satyanarayana Puja**: a prayer ceremony seeking blessings for the newly married couple's happiness and prosperity.
- **Balaji Kalyanam**: a traditional celestial-wedding ceremony of Lord Venkateswara (Balaji), held to bless the couple. Theirs is at the Hindu Temple of Atlanta.
- **Chaat**: tangy, crunchy Indian street-food snacks.
- **Pani puri (paani puri)**: crispy hollow puris filled with spiced water and fillings, eaten in one bite.
- **Chole bhature**: spiced chickpea curry served with fluffy fried bread.
- **Biryani**: fragrant layered rice dish cooked with spices.
- **Jalebi**: a bright orange, syrup-soaked spiral sweet.
- **Lehenga / Saree / Sherwani / Kurta**: traditional Indian outfits. Guests are welcome in Indian or Western attire as noted for each event.
